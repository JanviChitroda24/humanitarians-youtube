## This week's contribution
**Topic:** why noise-cancelling headphones work, for a viewer with no science background.
**What the video covers:** sound as changes in air pressure; a microphone picks up outside noise and the headphones play a sound with opposite pressure changes, so the noise at the ear becomes quieter. The path: outside noise → microphone → speaker → ear. Works best on steady sounds like an engine hum; voices and sudden sounds may still get through; ear cushions also block some sound. It reduces noise at the ear and does not silence the room or create perfect silence.
**Evidence:** the cancelled wave in the animation is computed, not drawn — an opposite wave 12% too weak and slightly mistimed leaves about 24% of the noise, shown on screen.
